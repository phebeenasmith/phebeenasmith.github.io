Job Market Paper

College Financial Aid Composition and Search Behavior

  • Abstract

    A quantitative overlapping-generations model with directed job search is developed to study how the composition of college financing shapes graduates' labor market outcomes. In my model, college can be financed by grants, loans, family transfers, or earnings, and graduates with more debt search for shorter periods and accept lower-paying jobs. Using NLSY97 data, I document that graduates with more student debt spend less time without work after college and earn lower wages, consistent with this mechanism. Expanding federal loan limits raises enrollment but increases graduate debt, shortening job search and lowering wages. In contrast, expanding grants allows graduates to search longer and secure higher-paying jobs, suggesting that grant-based aid improves labor market efficiency.
